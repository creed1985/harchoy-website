Bespoke Suit MOQ Explained: Why 50 Pieces Is the Sweet Spot

August 7, 2026 · 6 min read · Ordering & Manufacturing

Quick answer: MOQ (minimum order quantity) is the smallest number of suits a factory will produce in one order. Most bespoke suit factories require 100–300 pieces per style. Factories built for tailoring studios — including HARCHOY — accept 50-piece orders: enough to cover a full size run, small enough to keep your first wholesale investment manageable.

If you run a tailoring studio and you've ever asked a factory for a quote, the conversation almost always stalls at the same three letters: MOQ. The price per suit looks workable — then you see the minimum order is 300 pieces, and the whole plan collapses.

This guide explains what MOQ actually covers, why factories set it, how it shapes the price per suit, and why a 50-piece order is the practical entry point for most small studios and white-label brands.

What Does MOQ Mean in Suit Manufacturing?

MOQ is the smallest batch a factory agrees to produce for a single order. In bespoke and OEM suit manufacturing, the MOQ usually applies per style — one jacket-and-trouser model — while the pieces inside that batch can be split across sizes and, depending on the factory, across a few colors of the same fabric.

The figure is not arbitrary. It reflects the point at which a factory's fixed costs for your order — fabric sourcing, pattern-making, line setup, and quality control — stop making the job economically unviable.

Why Factories Set MOQs (It's Not Greed)

Every suit order carries fixed costs that exist whether you make 10 suits or 1,000:

  • Fabric minimums. Premium mills sell cloth in rolls with their own minimums. A single suit needs roughly 3 meters; sourcing one roll for a tiny order costs disproportionately more per meter than buying for a full batch.
  • Pattern-making and grading. Your style needs a base pattern, then grading into a full size range. This skilled work is the same effort for 10 pieces or 100.
  • Line setup. Cutting plans, thread and trims sourcing, machine configuration, and operator briefing all happen once per order.
  • Quality control. A structured QC process — at HARCHOY, a 10-point inspection per garment — takes the same per-order setup regardless of volume.

The MOQ is simply the batch size at which those fixed costs, spread across the pieces, still leave both sides a workable margin.

Typical MOQ Tiers in the Industry

ModelTypical MOQWho it suits
Made-to-measure (single client)1 pieceIndividual clients; high per-unit cost
Small-batch OEM for studios20–50 piecesTailoring studios, young white-label brands
Standard OEM production100–300 piecesEstablished brands with predictable demand
Large-scale manufacturing500+ piecesRetail chains, corporate uniform programs

The lower the tier, the higher the price per unit — because fixed costs are divided across fewer garments. The skill is finding the lowest volume at which your resale margin still works.

Why 50 Pieces Is the Sweet Spot

For a tailoring studio placing its first wholesale order, 50 pieces hits three targets at once:

1. It covers a real size curve

Fifty suits let you build a credible range — for example, sizes 46–54 EU weighted toward 48–52, the band most European and US clients fall into. Anything smaller forces you to skip sizes your clients will ask for.

2. It keeps the trial investment bounded

A first order is a test: of the factory's quality, communication, and delivery. Capping it at 50 pieces means the worst case stays affordable — which is why HARCHOY pairs the 50-piece MOQ with DDP delivery to 50+ countries, so there are no surprise costs on top.

3. It unlocks near-standard pricing

At 50 pieces, pattern and setup costs are amortized across enough units that the per-suit price lands in a range where resale margins are realistic. Scaling to 100+ later usually lowers the unit price further.

How to Start at the Minimum MOQ

If you want to enter at 50 pieces, structure the order to minimize complexity:

  1. One proven style. A classic two-piece single-breasted suit, not three experimental designs.
  2. Two core colors. Navy and charcoal cover the majority of business and occasion demand.
  3. One reliable fabric. A mid-weight wool (see our wool fabric guide) avoids multi-fabric minimums.
  4. A demand-weighted size curve. Concentrate pieces where your clients actually sit.
  5. DDP shipping terms. One fixed landed price, no customs surprises — our DDP guide explains why this matters most on first orders.

Common Questions About Suit MOQs

What is the minimum order quantity for bespoke suits?

Most factories require 100–300 pieces per style. Studios-focused manufacturers accept 50 pieces — the level HARCHOY works at — which covers a full size run while keeping the first-order investment controlled.

Can I order fewer than 50 suits?

Single made-to-measure pieces are possible, but the per-unit cost is high because all fixed costs land on one garment. That model works for individual clients, not for building resale inventory with margin.

Does MOQ apply per color or per size?

Per style, as a rule. Within the 50 pieces you can split across sizes and usually a small number of colors in the same fabric. More colors and fabrics raise the effective minimum.

Will my price per suit drop if I order more later?

Yes. Fixed costs are already amortized, and cutting and sewing efficiency improves with volume. Many partners start at 50, validate sell-through, then reorder at 100+.

What's the safest first order structure?

One style, two core colors, one mid-weight wool fabric, sizes weighted to your client base, shipped DDP. It's the configuration we recommend to every first-time partner.
